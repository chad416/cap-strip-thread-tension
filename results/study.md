# Study results

Generated by `scripts/run_study.py`. Everything here is **simulation**; no line data. Parameter status (read / abstract / std / derived / assumed) is in DOSSIER section 8.2 and in `captension.params`.

## 1. Plant numbers and plausibility

- Cord on a full bobbin: **15.0 km**, 4.51 kg (r 40 -> 110 mm, width 200 mm, fill 0.6); 4.2 h at 60 m/min.
- Guide gain G = exp(mu * wrap) = exp(0.3 x 1.6) = **1.616**; a position-to-position spread of mu of 0.04 gives a G spread of 6.4 % (1 sigma).
- Template drag per cord at 1.0 m/s: **2.97 N** (power law n = 0.2226: at 0.1 m/s it is still 1.78 N).
- Bobbin-side tension for 15 N at the template entrance: 9.28 N; brake torque 1.02 N m (full) to 0.37 N m (core); turndown 2.75.
- Effective span length L0 + G (L1 + L2) = 6.85 m; span stiffness EA/L = 88 N/m.

Span resonance (bobbin inertia against the cord span) and bobbin rotation frequency:

| radius | J (g m2) | span resonance f_n (Hz) | rotation at line speed (Hz) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 110 mm | 32.21 | 0.91 | 1.45 |
| 75 mm | 7.55 | 1.29 | 2.12 |
| 45 mm | 1.63 | 1.66 | 3.54 |

The rotation frequency (the eccentricity excitation) stays 1.6-2.4 times above the span resonance over the whole bobbin, so eccentricity is not resonant here. The resonance itself is lightly damped (cord damping only) and a magnetic-particle brake adds no speed-dependent torque: ramps, stops and stick-slip excite it (sections 3, 4 and 10).

## 6. Virtual (model-based) tension instead of a sensor

FB_ThreadBrake computes T_virtual = G_nom (M_est + J dw/dt + M_bear) / r_hat from the brake current, the encoder and the diameter estimate. Error against the true tension at the template entrance:

| alt. | mean static error per cord | p99 static error | dynamic RMS (steady) | p99 error during start ramp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | 7.2 % | 23.3 % | 1.0 % | 22.2 % |
| D | 7.0 % | 22.9 % | 1.1 % | 21.5 % |

The static error is the brake torque-per-ampere error (6.0 % 1 sigma), the residual drag and hysteresis, and the guide spread (G_nom is used for every position). None of these is observable from current and speed alone in steady running, so the virtual tension cannot replace the sensor for absolute accuracy (calibrated 3-roller sensor: 0.19 N 1 sigma, 1.3 % of set). It does track the inertia term during ramps and is useful as a plausibility check of the sensor and as the break detector of alternative C.

The diameter estimate starts at the old (near-empty) radius, 45 mm, on a 104 mm bobbin. v/omega disagrees by more than 15 %, so it is rejected and, after ReseedTime = 1 s of consistent readings, used to re-seed the estimate (FB_ThreadBrake, region Radius). - Calibrated chain: **0.19 N** (1 sigma) = 1.3 % of the set tension. Without in-situ calibration the +-1 % FS of a 49 N head alone is +-0.49 N = +-3.3 % of set.
- Range: highest tension at the sensor in any category-1 scenario of D 22.6 N, inside the 49 N range; resolution 1.8 mN per count.
- Bandwidth: 30 Hz head against a span resonance of 1-3 Hz and a 10 ms loop: adequate.
- Geometry: with 100 mm outer spacing and 10 mm deflection the head sees F = 0.392 T; the deflection adds two rotating rollers to the path (negligible friction), and the position after the last guide is the only place where each cord is still separate and already carries the guide friction (section 2, D vs D_pre).
